# Screens

What each of Mylder's six screens shows, and where every figure on it comes from. The figures rest on three things only: the whole-city snapshot every browser polls (one `journeyPos` answer a minute, joined to the timetable and given data states by the server; [`live-data.md`](live-data.md)), the prepared timetable ([`timetable.md`](timetable.md)), and a call made because a person opened a stop or a vehicle. Until the Rejseplanen key arrives, production runs on the timetable source, the default when `MYLDER_SOURCE` is unset ([ADR 0018](adr/0018-mylder-goes-public-on-the-timetable-and-siri-et-and-adds-the-api-with-the-key.md), [`live-data.md`](live-data.md)): it makes no call, so every vehicle is Timetable only, and each screen below says how it reads there, under "On the timetable source". The simulated Rejseplanen, which shows every data state, is chosen by name for development, the recordings and the demo. A figure the data can't back shows "—" and says why; nothing is averaged from a biased sample, and nothing is invented to fill a slot. The data-state rules are in [`data-states.md`](data-states.md) and the words in [`GLOSSARY.md`](../GLOSSARY.md); this page doesn't repeat them. Every screen is built from Perron, and this page names the components it uses; the changes Perron still needs, and each screen's stand-in until they ship, are listed in the spec rather than here, since that list shrinks as Perron ships.

The six screens are the moving map, vehicle detail, the stop panel, City pulse, the Vehicles list and Disruptions. On a desktop the map fills the window, City pulse, the Vehicles list and Disruptions are tabs in a side sheet, and vehicle detail and the stop panel open as a panel; the sheet, the panels and the highlight chip start below the page's header, the top bar however many rows it takes and the Latest now pill's row when it has one, so the header never covers their headings. On a phone the sheet is a bottom sheet with a peek, and the panels open from the bottom, in front of it, whether a tap or a link opened them. The About dialog is one tap from every screen. Everything is in English and Danish, light and dark.

## The moving map

**Shows.** Every trip on the map as a dot on its pattern's path, moving between polls: underway trips, Waiting trips standing at their first stop, Timetable only trips placed from the timetable, No signal trips moving on the timetable and their last delay. Each dot is drawn in its line's colour (its mode's colour for buses and trains) in the glyph of its data state, so the five states can be told apart without colour. Line paths and stops are drawn under the dots from the timetable's patterns, stops and paths. Out to zoom 13 a station is a dot that grows as the map zooms in, an interchange, a station of more than one rail mode such as Nørreport, a size up from a station of one. From zoom 13 in, a rail station is drawn as a transit map draws one: a rounded bar laid across every line that stops there and long enough to take in each line's strand, turned with the map, so a station is easy to find and tap. Where a station's lines run apart, such as København H's Metro beside its trains, each gets its own bar. A bus stop shows from zoom 14 and stays a small dot. The basemap is one forked style recoloured from Perron's map tokens into light and dark. The glyph treatment and the overlay mode are chosen from the map-layer prototype (Prototype the map layer with Perron, #14); the rest of the map's rendering is as settled in ADR 0003.

**The top bar** holds the brand, the Updated line, the filters, the Latest now pill, the language switch and the About button. The Updated line reads "Updated 08:14:05": the time the server received the position answer the snapshot was built from, with seconds, because the poll is a minute and the second matters for a dot's age. It is the one time the whole map moves on, so it is the only time in the top bar, and it never counts up: while the page is Disconnected it keeps the last snapshot's time, and while Paused it keeps the last answer's time and adds "paused". The mode filters are one chip per mode in the timetable (Bus, Metro, S-tog, Train, and Light rail where the timetable has it), each with the count of trips underway in that mode before filtering. A filter changes what is drawn and counted on every screen, never what the data says, and the sub-lines keep the whole in view ("of 1 312 in service"). After them, the Planes filter, "Planes 27", counts the aircraft the map draws ([`aircraft.md`](aircraft.md)); turned off, the planes leave the map, the browser stops asking for them and the chip drops its count, since it no longer knows it. Aircraft are never counted in City pulse or listed in the Vehicles list, so the Planes filter changes neither. The chip shows once the aircraft have answered, or while the address hides them, so it can turn them on again; with the aircraft off on the server it never shows. The chips' group is named "Filters", since it holds more than modes, and the address keeps every filter as `?hide=bus,aircraft`.

**Latest now** is one fact, always in view: the trip running furthest behind its timetable right now, anywhere on the map, whatever the filters, among the trips whose delay is known. The pill reads "Latest now [950R] +77 min at Slotspavillonen St.": the line badge, the delay in whole minutes with its sign, and the stop the vehicle is at or last passed; a screen reader hears "Biggest delay right now: 950R to Hillerød, 77 min late, between Slotspavillonen St. and Hillerød St.", "at" the stop where the vehicle stands at one. A press opens that trip's vehicle detail in the address, as a tap on its dot does. It is worked out in the browser from the snapshot and the clock alone, with no call and nothing kept between snapshots, and moves on with each snapshot. A trip counts while its data state by the clock is Live, Ageing or Estimated, with a delay and not cancelled; the latest is the largest delay, ties broken by the trip key so the order holds, and an early trip never ranks. The Metro and the light rail never appear on SIRI-ET's delays, which carry nothing of them. It stands out from 3 min late, the Delay component's late, counted in the whole minutes it shows. The pill tells the truth in every other state and then names no trip: "Latest now +2 min at most" when no trip is 3 min late; "Delays from Rejseplanen not updated since 09:21" when SIRI-ET's feed has sent nothing for 5 min, by the same rule as the data cards ("not received yet" before any message), so it never points at an old delay; and "Latest now —", "the Metro sends no delays", when no trip on the map shows a delay, as when only the Metro runs at night, or when Rejseplanen's answers, real, simulated or recorded, carry none. With the delays off, which is the timetable source with SIRI-ET's feed off, the one source that can send no delay at all, there is no pill. Wider than a phone it sits in the top bar's tools, before the language switch, whenever the bar, as it is laid out, has room for it on its one row with "Latest now", the badge, the delay and at least the first ten characters of the stop's name; the bar never wraps onto a second row for it, whatever the chips, their counts, the language or the Updated line say. Where the bar has no such room, as at 1280 px with six chips or wherever the bar already takes two rows, it takes a row of its own at the start under the bar, on glass of its own, as wide as its words and at most the bar's width, as it always does on a phone; the sheet and the panels start below that row. The page measures the bar again whenever it changes size or its words change, and moves the pill before it paints; it moves into the bar only with a few pixels to spare, and never tries the bar again at the same width and words once the bar wrapped with it, so it never rocks between the two. Wherever it sits, "Latest now", the badge and the delay stay whole and the stop's name is cut short with an ellipsis rather than push the pill past its room; the accessible name keeps the whole name. In Danish: "Mest forsinket nu", "Største forsinkelse lige nu: … min forsinket, mellem … og …", "højst +2 min" and "Metroen sender ingen forsinkelser".

**The legend** names the five data states with their glyphs, in the states' order, then, while the planes are on the map, the plane's glyph beside "Planes". If the first day with the key leaves Mylder on `CALC_REPORT` alone, no trip can be Live, and the legend says so once rather than every dot pretending." On the timetable source it adds no note: About names the source. While the aircraft feed fails and the planes are shown, it says since when there has been no data: "Planes: no data since 15:42".

**Map controls:** zoom in, zoom out, reset view, the 3D toggle, a compass, and a pause control that stops the dots moving (they then step to their new positions when each snapshot lands). The same stepping is used when the viewer's system asks for reduced motion. A scale bar sits beside the controls. Every dot has a tap target of at least 24 px.

**The 3D view** ([ADR 0016](adr/0016-3d-is-an-opt-in-view-with-the-dots-over-the-buildings.md)) is a view the rider chooses: the map opens flat and north-up, and the 3D toggle eases it to a tilt of about 50° and back to flat. Gestures tilt and turn it at any time too (a right-drag on a desktop, a two-finger drag on a phone), up to 60°, and the toggle shows pressed whenever the map is tilted, however it got there. Buildings rise from zoom 14, from OpenStreetMap's heights, in Perron's building colour, see-through at Perron's building opacity; the dots, the aircraft, the line paths and the stops are drawn over them, so a bus behind a block shows through it. Dots keep one size near and far. The aircraft stay at their ground position, their altitude in their card. The compass points north as the map turns, and a press turns the map back to north. Reset view returns to flat and north-up, as well as to the opening place. Follow and a highlight's fit move the centre and zoom and keep the rider's tilt and heading. The scale bar hides while the map is tilted, and shows again once it is flat, turned or not. The address keeps the heading and tilt as `map=zoom/lat/lon/bearing/pitch` while the map isn't flat, so a link opens in 3D. Under reduced motion the toggle and the compass jump rather than ease. On a phone, the 3D view waits for a measured frame rate with the buildings on: until then, under Perron's `breakpoint.phone` the toggle and the compass don't show and gestures don't tilt or turn the map, though a link with a tilt still opens tilted, which is how the frame rate is measured.

**The hover card** on a dot shows its line, its destination, its data state's word and its state label ("Reported 20 s ago", "Last seen 3 min ago", "Estimated from schedule", "Timetable only", "No signal since 10:42"), aged by the clock from the snapshot's times. A tap on a dot opens vehicle detail; a tap on a stop opens its departures.

**The plane's card** is a plane's hover card, and a tap on the plane opens the same text in a small panel, kept in the address as `?aircraft=<hex>` and closed as every detail panel is: Esc, its close button, or another vehicle, stop or plane opened in its place. It reads the aircraft snapshot ([`aircraft.md`](aircraft.md)), so it asks no route of its own; an open panel keeps the aircraft's poll running, even while the Planes filter hides the planes, so a link to a plane with the planes hidden still shows it, and hiding them leaves the open panel ageing and moving on. It shows the callsign, or the ICAO hex of a plane that sends none, then "A20N · 3 350 m · 593 km/h": the type code, the altitude in metres (the feed's feet × 0.3048, to the nearest 10 m) or "On the ground", and the ground speed in km/h (knots × 1.852, to the km/h), each left out where the feed gives none. Then its data state's word and label, aged by the clock from its position's time: "Reported 4 s ago" (Live), "Last seen 40 s ago" (Ageing), "Placed by ground receivers 6 s ago" (Estimated) or "No signal since 15:42" (No signal, the position's time on Copenhagen's clock). No route, origin or destination: the feed carries none. A tap on a plane wins over a stop under it. Once the plane has left the snapshot, or its position is old enough to drop, the panel says "Plane not on the map". Both are real text a screen reader reads, in English and Danish, which writes the speed in km/t.

**The highlight chip** appears while something is highlighted, "Showing: Signal fault at Danshøj · Clear", from Show whole line in vehicle detail or Show on map on a disruption. Clearing it returns the map to normal.

**The corner credit** reads "Transit data: Rejseplanen (CC BY 4.0) · © OpenStreetMap contributors · © OpenMapTiles · OpenFreeMap", each a link, with "Delays: Rejsekort & Rejseplan A/S" after the transit data while SIRI-ET's delays are on, unlinked and with no licence until Rejseplanen says which applies to its SIRI-ET datasets, whose pages say "Licence: None", and with "Aircraft: © adsb.lol contributors under the ODbL", or "Aircraft: © adsb.fi" when adsb.fi answered, after the transit data once the page has aircraft (`docs/aircraft.md`), at the bottom right on a desktop and above the sheet's peek on a phone, or an open stop sheet's taller one, wrapping to two lines and never hidden behind a button. The Rejseplanen link leads to the About dialog, so a phone reader can read the full terms in one tap.

**Page notices**, as alerts at the top of the map, above every panel:

- **Disconnected**, when the browser's own polls of Mylder fail: "No connection since 08:16. Vehicles move on the last data from 08:14:05." It appears at once, because the browser knows at once. The dots keep moving on what the browser has, and every state ages by the clock.
- **Paused**, when Mylder's server gets no position answers from Rejseplanen, never on the timetable source, which asks it nothing: "Rejseplanen hasn't answered since 08:09." when calls fail or answer nothing, shown once the latest answer is older than the Live limit; or "Live data is paused until 1 November: this month's calls are used up." when the month's budget is spent, shown at once, since the server knows. Dots keep moving; states age by the rules in [`data-states.md`](data-states.md).
- **The budget line**, when the poll has stretched: "Updating every 90 s to stay within the data budget." (or "2 min"), in the About dialog and at the head of City pulse, never on a dot.
- **The timetable notice**, when the prepared timetable has under 7 days of service left, and the recorded-data banner on a preview deployment.
- **The source banner**, whenever the transit isn't real: "Simulated data: vehicles and times are made up from the timetable." or "Recorded data from 16 June 2026.", followed, once the page has aircraft, by where they come from: "Planes: live from adsb.lol." (or adsb.fi, whichever answered) or "Planes: recorded adsb.lol data.". The timetable source, with SIRI-ET's delays or without, has no banner: the legend's note, About and the panels say every vehicle is placed from its timetable. Recorded planes beside real transit, Rejseplanen's or the timetable's, get a banner of their own; live ones none.

When both Disconnected and Paused hold, Disconnected is shown and the Paused notice from the last snapshot is kept beneath it.

**Accessibility.** The canvas has an accessible name ("Live map of Copenhagen"). The dots themselves are not reachable by keyboard; the Vehicles list is the text alternative, and every vehicle on the map is a row there. Keys: + and − zoom, arrows pan, Shift + ← and → turn the map, Shift + ↑ and ↓ tilt it, Esc closes panels; the About dialog lists them.

**Built from:** the dots and paths on the canvas read Perron's tokens directly, and the planes Perron's `aircraft` icon and its tokens (no component); Brand, Surface (glass), FilterChip with Count, IconButton, Button, LineBadge and Delay (the Latest now pill), VehicleDotLegend, MapControls, ScaleBar, Attribution, MapTooltip, HighlightChip, Alert, Toast, Text, Link, Dialog and Kbd, under PerronProvider; the plane's panel, DetailPanel with VehicleDotLabel, Text and EmptyState.

## Vehicle detail

**Shows.** A panel for one trip, opened from a dot, a Vehicles list row or a departure row.

- **Header:** the line badge, "to Destination", and a subheader with the mode and the operator ("S-tog · DSB"), from the timetable. No vehicle name: the glossary's fleet number is shown only when a source reports one, and none does.
- **State block:** the state's dot and word, and one line per state: Live, "Reported by the vehicle; seen 08:14:05" (the time of the answer that last carried it); Ageing, "Last reported 08:09; moving on the timetable since."; Estimated, "Estimated from the timetable and a delay of +3 min" or "Position computed by Rejseplanen"; Timetable only, "No realtime for this trip today; placed from the timetable.", or on the timetable source, where no trip has realtime because none is asked for, "No live positions yet; placed from the timetable."; No signal, "No signal since 08:09; moving on the timetable and its last delay."; a Metro trip, "Runs to an interval". A cancelled trip's panel says "Cancelled".
- **Facts:** Delay, Last seen, Next stop. The delay is Rejseplanen's own, expected minus timetabled at the trip's next call, from `journeyDetail` while the panel is open (one call, cached a minute) or from the snapshot when the answer carries delays; "—" for Timetable only and No signal; "Runs to an interval" for the Metro. Last seen is the answer time for Live and Ageing, "—" otherwise. There is no speed: the API gives none, and two positions a minute apart give a mean that is wrong at every stop.
- **Where:** "Between Nørreport and Kongens Nytorv" or "At Nørreport", read from the position's place on the pattern; "Last seen near Nørreport" for No signal; "Waiting at Hundige, departs 10:42" for a Waiting trip, never "At Hundige" unless a report puts it there.
- **Next stops:** the calls ahead with their timetabled times and a delay per call from `journeyDetail`, or the snapshot's delay for every later call until the detail has loaded, the next stop marked. For a No signal trip the times are the timetable's with "Delay unknown". Cancelled calls are marked. A Metro trip lists its calls without times, with the interval instead.
- **Follow** keeps the camera on the dot and ends when the trip reaches its last stop, when the viewer pans, or when the panel closes. **Show whole line** fits the trip's pattern and raises the highlight chip.
- **Messages:** the trip's own messages from `journeyDetail`, as cards after the next stops, with their stop range marked on the next-stops list: the first and last stop it covers carry a note after the name, "Sydhavn — Langsom kørsel from here". This is the one honest tie from a message to a trip, and it costs no extra call.
- **Source line:** "Rejseplanen · position 08:14:05 · times 08:14:20" when `journeyDetail` was called; "Rejseplanen · position 08:14:05 · times from the timetable" when not; the messages carry "checked 08:15".

When on-demand calls are stopped by the budget, or the API is failing, the panel is served from the timetable plus the snapshot's delay and says "From the timetable".

**On the timetable source** (`MYLDER_SOURCE=timetable`), which asks Rejseplanen nothing, every trip is Timetable only: the stops list their planned times, with no delay and no expected time, the Delay fact reads "—" (a Metro trip's "Runs to an interval"), and the source line reads "Timetable · placed 08:14:05", the time of the snapshot the trip was placed in. The panel says nothing of Rejseplanen and has no "From the timetable", since the timetable is the source, not a fallback. Follow and Show whole line work as on any source.

**With SIRI-ET's delays** (`MYLDER_UPDATES`, spec #146), a trip Rejseplanen has sent an update for is Estimated: "Estimated from the timetable and a delay of +3 min". Its stops keep their planned times with the expected time each call's delay gives marked "~" ("09:17 ~09:23"), the times the map moves it on, and each call it skips is marked Cancelled. Its Delay fact is the delay at its next stop, late ("+3 min", heard "3 min late") or early ("−2 min", "2 min early"), so it can differ from the delay the trip ends on. Its source line reads "Timetable and Rejseplanen's delays · updated 08:14:05", the time its latest update was received. A trip with no update, and every Metro trip, reads as on the timetable source.

**Built from:** DetailPanel (Sheet and DetailHeader), VehicleDotLabel, FactList, StopTimeline, Button, Delay, DisruptionCard, Text.

## Stop panel

**Shows.** A panel for one stop, opened from a stop on the map or a disruption card, with the stop's name, its lines as badges, the trips standing at its platforms now, and below them its departures and its arrivals for the next 30 minutes across its platforms, as two tabs.

- **At the platforms now** ("Ved perronerne nu"), above the departures: the trips [standing](../GLOSSARY.md) at the stop's platforms at this moment, in the modes the filters show, soonest shown departure first, each a row as a departure's with its line, destination, track when the board gives one, countdown and time to its departure, delay and data state; a tap opens its vehicle. They spend no call: the server works them out from the stop's calls in the timetable and the snapshot, as the stop's answer is built. A trip stands between its shown arrival and its shown departure there, the times its data state shows; a Waiting trip at its first stop here stands from 5 minutes before it leaves; and a trip that ends here stands for 2 minutes after its shown arrival, its row reading "Ends here" with no countdown, since no source says when it leaves the platform. The tracker keeps such a trip's delay that long after it has left the map, and its row is Estimated, as a departure with realtime off the map is. Where the stop's departure board is in hand, its expected times and tracks win over the snapshot's. The Metro is never listed, so its 20-second stops don't flicker in and out. At most 4 rows show, then "Show all 6", which shows the rest and turns to "Show fewer"; with none standing, the section says "No vehicle of the types shown is standing at the platforms now." On a phone, whose panel is a bottom sheet, the same trips are one row of cards the rider swipes through sideways, every one of them with no "Show all", each card the same row with its line, destination, track, time to its departure and data state, and a tap on it opening its vehicle; the next card peeks in at the end of the row, and the Departures and Arrivals tabs sit below it. The sheet's peek reaches down to the end of the tabs, standing row or not, never less than Perron's `size.sheet-peek`, so a rider sees the tabs before any drag; it follows the row as trips come and go, though never under a finger dragging the sheet, and the map's foot, its corner credit with it, stands on that peek. The row is a list named "At the platforms now", and Tab moves from card to card, scrolling each into view.
- **The tabs**, below the standing trips: **Departures** ("Afgange") and **Arrivals** ("Ankomster"), Perron's Tabs, following the ARIA tabs pattern: the arrow keys move between them, Enter or Space opens one, and each tab's panel is announced by its name. Departures opens first. The open tab is in the address, `?stop=<id>&board=arrivals` for Arrivals and no `board` for Departures, so a shared link opens on the same tab; opening another stop starts it on Departures again. Each tab has its own messages, note and source line.
- **Departures**:
  - **The list** comes from one `multiDepartureBoard` call for the stop's platforms when it opens, cached a minute; when on-demand calls are stopped or the API is failing, from the timetable with the snapshot's delay where a trip has one. Each row shows the line, the destination, a countdown from the expected time (or the timetabled time when there is none; under 45 s it reads "Now"), the timetabled time struck through when the two differ by a minute or more, the platform or track when the board gives one, the delay, and the trip's data state, joined to the snapshot by the trip's key. A row whose trip is not in the snapshot but carries realtime shows as Estimated, since its expected time is realtime without a position. A departure's time follows its trip's state as [`data-states.md`](data-states.md) sets out: "~" before an Estimated time, and for Timetable only and No signal the timetabled time with the delay unknown, "—".
  - **The Metro:** an interval row, "every 3 min" or "every 3 to 4 min", worked out from the planned departures in the current hour in that direction, with no countdown and no clock time, and the note "Metro: planned times". If the first calls with the key show Metro realtime, Metro rows become ordinary rows.
  - **Cancelled** rows stay, marked Cancelled, with the countdown struck.
  - **Empty:** "No departures in the next 30 minutes for the vehicle types shown."
  - **Messages**, above the list: the messages on the stop's board, and the disruption list's whose stops include this one, as cards; both come with the stop's answer, so no call of their own.
  - **Source line:** "Rejseplanen · departures 08:14:20" when the board was called; "Timetable and positions 08:14:05" when served from the timetable plus the snapshot's delays.
- **Arrivals**: the trips [arriving](../GLOSSARY.md) at the stop's platforms in the next 30 minutes, the same list seen from the other side. They are a second answer of the stop, of the same shape as its departures, asked for only while the Arrivals tab is open, and again as each falls due.
  - **The list** comes from one `multiArrivalBoard` call for the stop's platforms when the tab opens, cached a minute apart from the departure board; when on-demand calls are stopped or the API is failing, from the timetable with the snapshot's delay where a trip has one. Each row shows the line, where the trip comes from ("From Holte St."), the platform or track when a board gives one, a countdown to its arrival, the timetabled time struck through beside the expected one a minute or more away, the delay and the trip's data state, by the departures' rules: "~" before an Estimated time, the delay unknown, "—", for Timetable only and No signal, and "Now" under 45 s. A trip's first stop is never an arrival, and a trip that ends here is. A row opens its vehicle, as a departure does.
  - **The Metro:** an interval row, "every 3 min", worked out from the planned arrivals in the current hour in that direction, with where the next comes from, no countdown and no clock time, and the note "Metro: planned times".
  - **Cancelled** rows stay, marked Cancelled, with the countdown struck.
  - **Empty:** "No arrivals in the next 30 minutes for the vehicle types shown."
  - **Messages**, above the list: the messages on the stop's arrival board, and the disruption list's whose stops include this one.
  - **Source line:** "Rejseplanen · arrivals 08:14:20" when the arrival board was called; "Timetable and positions 08:14:05" when served from the timetable plus the snapshot's delays, the positions' time of the snapshot its times were shifted by.
- **Note**, under each tab's list: "Countdowns use Rejseplanen's expected times where it has them; the rest use the timetable, and say so." Arrivals, while their times come from the timetable, as when on-demand calls are stopped, read instead "Countdowns use the timetable, moved by the delay the positions show where a trip has one; the rest use the timetable alone, and say so."; an answer from a board carries the first note on either tab.
- **On the timetable source** (`MYLDER_SOURCE=timetable`), which asks Rejseplanen nothing, both tabs list every trip as Timetable only at its planned time with the delay unknown, "—", and the Metro as its interval, and each tab's note reads "Countdowns use the timetable alone; live times aren't connected yet." and its source line "Timetable · 08:14:05", the time of the answer; no "From the timetable", no "Timetable and positions", nothing of Rejseplanen.
- **With SIRI-ET's delays** (`MYLDER_UPDATES`, spec #146), a trip with an update is Estimated, its row's expected time marked "~" beside its planned time struck when they differ by a minute or more, and its delay; a trip with none keeps its planned time with the delay unknown, "—"; a cancelled trip's row, or the row of a trip that skips the stop, says Cancelled; the Metro keeps its interval. Each tab's note reads "Countdowns use the timetable, moved by Rejseplanen's delay where a trip has one; the rest use the timetable alone." and its source line "Timetable and Rejseplanen's delays · updated 08:14:05", the time of the delays' last message.

**Built from:** DetailPanel (stop variant) with LineBadges in its subheader, Section, Tabs, DepartureRow, Button, DataStatePanel, DisruptionCard, Text. A standing row is a DepartureRow, whose status part draws the trip's data state as VehicleDotLabel does, its dot and its word. On a phone, each standing card is that DepartureRow on a raised Surface.

**Shows.** Three key figures, a meter per mode, the day's profile, and the five states with their counts. Every count respects the mode filters and ages by the clock between polls, from the same pure rules the dots use, so the figures and the map never disagree.

## The Vehicles list

**Shows.** Every trip on the map as a row, so the list is the text alternative to the canvas: about 1,300 to 1,600 rows at the morning peak. Columns: Line, Destination, Position (the state's dot and word) and Delay. There is no age column, because the API gives no report time and an age would be the poll's age; the state word says enough in a list, and the detail panel shows "Last seen". There is no fleet number.

- **Delay** is shown when the snapshot carries delays for the city; if the first day shows it doesn't, the column is hidden rather than filled with "—" for all but the trips someone happened to open.
- **Sort:** by Line (modes in the order Metro, S-tog, Train, Bus, then line names in numeric order), by Destination, by Delay (unknown delays last whichever way) or by Position (state order, then Live before Ageing by the time last seen). The order stays steady between polls, so rows don't jump under a finger.
- **Search** over line name and destination. "No vehicles match." when nothing does.
- **A row** opens the vehicle's detail. The list re-renders when a snapshot lands and when a row's state ages, not every frame.
- **Count:** the Vehicles tab's count.
- **Source line:** "Rejseplanen · positions 08:14:05", above the rows, so it is seen before scrolling; on the timetable source, "Timetable · placed 08:14:05", where every row is Timetable only and the Delay column is hidden, unless SIRI-ET's delays are on: then a trip with an update is Estimated and the column gives its delay at its next call, as its panel does.

**Built from:** SearchField, Select (the sort), VehicleTable, DataStatePanel, Text.

## Disruptions

**Shows.** One list of cards, in this order:

1. **Mylder's own data cards**, at most one each, read from the snapshot and the clock by one rule in the domain: first, "Delays from Rejseplanen not updated since 09:56" (SIRI-ET's feed is on and its last message is 5 min old, or "not received yet" when none has come; on any source, and gone at the next message, [`data-states.md`](data-states.md), "When the answers stop"); "Live data paused until the 1st" (the budget is spent); "No answer from Rejseplanen since 08:09" (the API has failed or answered nothing for 5 min); "No bus positions since 08:09" (a mode that an answer carried earlier in the service day, with trips underway now, has had none for 5 min, one card per mode, never for a mode that doesn't report, such as the Metro while it is an interval line, and not while the card above holds); "Disruption messages not updated since 07:45" (the last `himsearch` call failed and the list is older than 15 min, or the list is more than an hour old however it was checked, as in a spent month). Disconnected is not a card: it is the browser's own condition and shows as the page notice.
2. **Rejseplanen's messages active now**, from `himsearch`, called every 15 minutes while anyone watches (every 30 at the tight budget level), on the same cached route as the snapshot. Sorted by priority if the first day shows priority orders messages, else by start time, newest first.
3. **Later:** messages starting within the next 7 days, under a heading, by start time.

A message belongs to Copenhagen when it names at least one stop in Mylder's stop set, or names no stops and its products overlap the modes Mylder shows. One naming only stops outside the box is left out. When an answer holds exactly 100 messages, the footer says "Rejseplanen sends at most 100 messages, so the list may be incomplete."

**A card** shows its kind (Delays, Diversion, Planned works, Data feed, or Service notice for a message whose fields don't say), read from the message's structured fields only: Planned works for a message that starts in the future or spans more than a day; Delays or Diversion only where the category or icon values the first day reveals mean them. Then the message's heading, its lead, and its full text behind "More" when longer, all as Rejseplanen wrote it, in Danish, in both languages, with the caption "From Rejseplanen, in Danish" in the English UI. Its modes as tags. "Affects Nørreport, Kongens Nytorv and 3 more" or "Between Hellerup and Klampenborg" from its stops. Its time, "From 08:03" or "24 to 25 October". And **Show on map**, which rings each affected stop in the card's severity colour, draws the stretch between its from and to stops along every pattern that runs through both, fits the camera to them and raises the highlight chip. A message with no stops has no Show on map.

No line badges, unless the first day shows the answers name lines in a field: a line read out of prose ("Linje 2A kører en anden rute") would often work and sometimes mislead, and a wrong badge on a disruption is worse than none. For the same reason the prototype's per-line "Running normally" list is gone: with messages naming stops, no line can honestly be called untouched. When no message is active, the last check succeeded and the list is up to date, the screen shows "Running normally" once, for the network, with the time it was checked.

Cancellations make no card. They are shown where they are seen: a cancelled row in a stop's departures, a cancelled trip's panel, cancelled calls on a trip's next stops. A card per cancellation would describe where people looked, not the network.

**Footer:** "Messages from Rejseplanen, checked 08:15. Checked every 15 min while the map is open." After a failed check, "Last checked 07:45; the latest check failed." After an hour without a successful check the list stays, marked "May be out of date", and the data card above says so. The time is the server's receipt time of the last good answer.

**On the timetable source** (`MYLDER_SOURCE=timetable`) no message is asked for, since `himsearch` needs the key, and nothing it doesn't ask for can be missing: in place of the list, its footer and Rejseplanen's gaps, the screen says "Disruption messages come with Rejseplanen's live connection, which isn't connected yet.", and the About dialog's Disruptions fact reads "Not connected yet". The one card it can show is a quiet SIRI-ET feed's, with SIRI-ET's delays on, above that sentence; the tab counts that card while it shows, and nothing otherwise.

**Built from:** DisruptionCard, SeverityLabel, Tag, NormalService, Button, Accordion, Section, Separator, DataStatePanel, Text.

On a phone a sheet or panel can cover the map and its corner credit, so each panel's own source line is what keeps the terms met there, and the About dialog is one tap away. ## Not altering the data

Rejseplanen's guidelines ask that its data not be altered so that it differs from the original or can be misread. What that means on each screen, so a builder can check it:

- A computed position never counts as Live; the dot's glyph and its word say which it is.
- Rejseplanen's messages are shown in its words and its language, with a caption in the English UI; no translation, no summary, no line read out of the prose.
- A delay is Rejseplanen's own, expected minus timetabled, to the minute it gives. Mylder's own placements say "Estimated" or "~", its day profile says "Planned, from today's timetable", and the shift that keeps a dot from jumping back at the next answer is never shown as a delay or a time.
- A figure the data can't back shows "—" and the reason, never a stand-in number; nothing is averaged from the stops and vehicles people happened to open.
- Mode filters, sorting and search change what is shown, not what the data says; the "of N in service" sub-line keeps the whole in view.
- The Metro's planned clock times are never shown as departures, because the trains don't keep them; the interval is what the Metro itself tells passengers.
- A time is the time of one answer, never a blend, and never counts up while the page is Disconnected.
