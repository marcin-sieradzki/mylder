# Live data

How Mylder gets positions from Rejseplanen API 2.0 to the browser on a free key, and what the browser receives. The data states themselves are defined in [`data-states.md`](data-states.md), and the planes' own feed and snapshot in [`aircraft.md`](aircraft.md); this page covers the plumbing: the one call a minute, the snapshot every browser polls, the caches between them, the small server store and the call budget. The words are in [`GLOSSARY.md`](../GLOSSARY.md).

Every number here is a starting value. The first day with the key settles the ones marked "first day", and a month of running settles the rest.

## The shape in one paragraph

Browsers never call Rejseplanen. Each browser polls one route of the app, which answers with one snapshot of every trip on the map for the whole city. The CDN in front of the app caches that snapshot until the next one is due, so a thousand browsers cost one request to the app a minute. When the cached snapshot expires and a browser asks for a new one, the app's tracker takes a one-minute lease in a small hosted Redis, calls Rejseplanen once, joins the answer to the timetable, decides each trip's data state, stores the snapshot and returns it. Nobody watching means no browser asks, so no call is made. The API is called only while someone watches, without a scheduler, a watcher count or an always-on process.

## Until the key: the timetable source

Until the Rejseplanen key arrives, production runs on the timetable source, `MYLDER_SOURCE=timetable`, which is also what an unset or empty `MYLDER_SOURCE` reads as, so a deployment that forgets the variable shows the honest map, never invented realtime ([ADR 0018](adr/0018-mylder-goes-public-on-the-timetable-and-siri-et-and-adds-the-api-with-the-key.md), [`testing-seams.md`](testing-seams.md)). It keeps everything on this page but the calls. The run still takes its lease once a minute while someone watches, builds the snapshot, stores it and serves it through the same caches on the same 60 s poll, but it calls nothing: no `journeyPos`, no `himsearch`, and an opened stop or vehicle answers from the timetable with no on-demand call. Its API layer refuses any request that reaches it, logging an error that names the source, so a regression that starts calling fails visibly instead of falling back unseen.

Every trip on the map, Waiting or underway, is Timetable only, placed by its planned times along its pattern's path ([`data-states.md`](data-states.md), "Where a trip is drawn"), as the Metro is on every source. The snapshot names its source `timetable`, its position mode is none whatever `MYLDER_POSITION_MODE` says, the API health stays `ok`, the budget `normal` with no call counted, last seen by mode empty and disruptions null. The run's log line reports no calls, the trips per mode and per state and the timetable's age. The source's store keys are its own, so switching to `rejseplanen` starts from a clean history and a zero call counter. What each screen says on it is in [`screens.md`](screens.md).

Going live stays one environment change: the key in `REJSEPLANEN_KEY` and `MYLDER_SOURCE=rejseplanen` in Production ("First day with the key", below). The simulated Rejseplanen stays for development, the recordings and the GitHub Pages demo, chosen by name with `MYLDER_SOURCE=simulated`.

## SIRI-ET: delays and cancellations before the key

Rejseplanen's SIRI-ET brings every trip's expected times and cancellations for Movia, DSB, the S-tog and Lokaltog, pushed over AMQP from Dataudveksleren to one always-on listener, which keeps the **update table** and serves it over HTTPS for the tracker to fetch ([ADR 0019](adr/0019-siri-et-brings-delays-and-cancellations-through-one-always-on-listener.md), spec #146). The research is in [`researches/2026-10-31-siri-et-listener.md`](researches/2026-10-31-siri-et-listener.md). The figures below were measured by the probe of 31 October 2026, a Saturday morning, and by replaying its recording, `recordings/siri-et/` (09:00 to 09:56 Copenhagen time), into the reader with `scripts/siri-et/replay-siri-et.ts`. A Saturday is quiet, so they are a floor for a weekday.

### The feed

| Dataset      | Messages a second | XML a second | Median message      | Journeys a message | Calls a journey update (median) | Between a journey's updates (median, 90th percentile) |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ------------ | ------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 785 DSB      | 0.4 to 0.6        | about 2 KB   | 3 KB                | 1.6                | 2                               | 50 s, 4 min                                           |
| 788 Movia    | 0.2 to 0.3        | about 39 KB  | 125 KB (max 930 KB) | 35                 | 9                               | 49 s, 2 min                                           |
| 799 Lokaltog | 0.1 to 0.2        | about 0.5 KB | 3.5 KB              | 1.1                | 4                               | 2 min, 6 min                                          |
| 801 S-tog    | 0.2 to 0.3        | about 1 KB   | 2 KB                | 1.4                | 1                               | 71 s, 5 min                                           |

- **Rates and sizes.** About 45 KB of XML a second in all, 95 % of it Movia's, which batches about 35 journeys a message: perhaps 100 to 150 KB/s at a weekday peak, 4 to 8 GB a day. Bodies are single-line SIRI 2.0 XML, uncompressed. From queued to received took under 25 ms at the median and 1.9 s at most.
- **Backlog kept while away.** A subscription keeps its messages while nobody reads them: a 10-minute gap was delivered in full within 30 s of reconnecting, and the first connection received everything since the subscription was made, 40 to 55 minutes earlier. No snapshot is sent on subscribe. So a restarted listener catches up from the backlog, and each message's `receivedAt` in a recording is its queued time, not when the probe pulled it.
- **Identifiers.** A journey is `DataFrameRef` (the service day; every one 2026-10-31) and `DatedVehicleJourneyRef` (`2727-8600351300000`). `LineRef` is the public line name for buses, S-tog and Lokaltog (`68`, `C`, `950R`) and the train number for DSB, whose `PublishedLineName` reads `IC 50118`. `TrainNumberRef` is on every journey, buses too. `OriginRef` and `DestinationRef` name the first and last stops even when their calls are not in the message. `StopPointRef` equals the timetable's platform or stop id without leading zeros, as it is: every DSB, S-tog and Lokaltog ref matches, and 6,969 of Movia's 7,120. Movia's misses are 98 nine-digit refs starting `980`, on lines 102A, 216, 315, 215, 318, 254, 848 and 847, and 53 stop numbers the timetable lacks, 46 on line 327. `SubscriptionRef` and `SubscriberRef` name the access point's feeds (`movia-et`, `DK-NAP-SIRI`), not the account's subscription.
- **Merged calls.** A message carries only some of a journey's calls, often not its first: a median of 9 for Movia and 1 or 2 for the trains. `IsCompleteStopSequence` is true on 944 of the recording's 22,431 journey updates, which then carry the whole trip. So the reader merges each message's calls into what it holds for the journey, a call replacing the one at the same stop and aimed time, and never replaces the journey: merged, a Movia journey holds a median of 29 calls. A later call can undo an earlier one's cancellation, as bus 68's Gyldenlandsvej was cancelled at 09:01 and served again at 09:08.
- **Cancellations.** 9 whole journeys (7 Lokaltog, 2 Movia) and 224 cancelled calls in the 56 minutes, each a `Cancellation` element on the journey or on the call.

### The reader

The reader (`src/siri-et/reader.ts`) takes each message, with its dataset and received time, and gives the update table at any moment. It reads a body in one pass over its tags, ignoring elements it doesn't use. It holds every journey it hears of, since a later message may bring a call inside the box, and lists those that call inside it: at any held call, or at their first or last stop. A call's delay is its expected minus its aimed departure, or arrival where it has no departure, in seconds; a call with no expected time gives none.

- **Dropped:** a journey 10 minutes after its expected arrival at its last stop (or, with no expected time, its aimed one), once the reader holds that call: the call at `DestinationRef`, which a round trip only reaches, never leaves. A journey heard only through calls short of its last stop stays, with what it has, until its service day has ended, and so does one with no calls. A service day starts as the timetable counts it (`serviceDayStart` in `src/timetable/time.ts`, midnight but on the two days the clocks change) and has ended 36 hours later: the longest in the prepared timetable of 1 November 2026 runs 34 h 9 min, a train 083 into 10:09 the next morning. So Saturday's journeys are all gone at noon on Sunday.
- **Limits:** 20,000 journeys held, past which the one updated longest ago is dropped and counted (`evicted`), and 4,000,000 characters a message, four times the largest seen; a longer message is counted and skipped. A body whose tags don't nest, or whose root isn't `Siri`, is counted as malformed and skipped whole; a journey without its service day (or with one that isn't a date), journey ref, line, origin or destination is counted and skipped, and the message's others are read.
- **Speed and memory:** the whole recording, 2,090 messages and 114 MB of XML, replays in about 3 s, and the reader holds its 1,286 journeys in about 13 MB.

### The update table

The table (`src/siri-et/table.ts`) is versioned (`version: 1`) and checked with one schema by the listener that writes it and the tracker that reads it. Its envelope carries when it was written, the last message's received time, each dataset's last message time and counts (messages, journey updates, malformed, too large, skipped journeys), the listener's version and start time, the timetable version its box used, and the journeys held and evicted. Each journey carries its dataset, service day, journey ref, line, operator, origin, destination and train number; when its latest message was received; whether it is cancelled; its first held call, so a journey with no delay can still be joined; its cancelled calls; and its delays as steps, each a call (stop and aimed time) and a delay, listed only where the delay changes from the call before. A step's delay holds for every call after it until the next step.

Replayed, the table grows as journeys come into their hour before departure:

| At (Copenhagen) | Journeys listed | Steps  | Cancelled | Cancelled calls | JSON   | Gzipped |
| --------------- | --------------- | ------ | --------- | --------------- | ------ | ------- |
| 09:05           | 491             | 4,774  | 1         | 9               | 359 KB | 45 KB   |
| 09:30           | 917             | 9,311  | 5         | 37              | 690 KB | 87 KB   |
| 09:56           | 1,282           | 11,595 | 7         | 38              | 895 KB | 112 KB  |

At 09:56 the listed journeys are 1,082 Movia, 97 S-tog, 64 DSB and 39 Lokaltog. Movia's delays wobble a minute either way from call to call, so its journeys average 10 steps; 49 of the S-tog's journeys and 16 of DSB's have no expected time yet, so no step. The table is about four times the 15 to 25 KB spec #146 budgeted for a weekday peak, and steps are most of it: written whole each minute, it would take about 4.8 GB a month of the store's free 10 GB on a quiet Saturday's figures, before the tracker reads it back. So the store doesn't carry it: the listener serves it on its own route and the tracker fetches it (ADR 0019, amended for #217), below.

### The tracker's use of the table

With `MYLDER_UPDATES` on (`docs/testing-seams.md`, "The update switch"), each run reads the table once, fetched from the listener or from the reader over a recording, and joins each journey to a trip of its service day (`DataFrameRef`) in `src/tracker/join.ts`:

- **Trains first by number.** A journey whose `TrainNumberRef`, without leading zeros, is the train number of a train or S-tog trip, and whose line is that trip's line or the number itself (DSB's), is that trip. Movia's `TrainNumberRef` is a vehicle journey number, never a train's, and its buses have none in the timetable.
- **Then by line and ends.** Otherwise the trips of its line from its `OriginRef`'s Stop to its `DestinationRef`'s are the candidates, and the first not yet joined that has a call at one of the journey's held calls (its first, its steps' and its cancelled ones) at that call's aimed time is the trip.
- **Unmatched.** A journey no trip fits, such as an Extra trip's or one whose first or last stop lies outside the timetable's box, is counted by its dataset's mode (788 bus, 785 and 799 train, 801 S-tog) in the snapshot's `updates.unmatched` and the run's log line, and not drawn. On the recording at 09:30, 22 of Movia's 772 journeys in the table don't join: 20 of line 19, whose stop 56543 the timetable, cut to its box, doesn't have, and 2 of 930R. Every train and S-tog journey joins by number.

A joined journey with a delay, a cancellation or a cancelled call is the trip's update: its received time is the trip's `updated`, each step's call leaves that many seconds late and the calls between take the step before's delay, so the trip's next calls and its place on the path follow its timetable plus its delay, and its cancelled calls are listed by index. The snapshot carries the update's steps on the trip, `steps`, each a call index and its delay in seconds, so an opened trip's calls and an opened stop's rows are timed by them as the map moves the trip, not by one delay for every call; the trip's `delay` is the one at its next call by those steps, the delay its panel gives, so the Vehicles list and City pulse read the same. A cancelled trip stays in the snapshot, marked `cancelled`, for the departure lists, and the map leaves it out. A joined journey with no expected time yet, as about half the S-tog's are, tells its trip nothing and leaves it Timetable only, as a departure row without a prognosis does. The trip's history keeps, for the rest of its service day, its latest `updated`, its last update's steps, and whether that update cancelled it and which calls, so a run without the table, such as one whose fetch failed or one on a quiet SIRI-ET feed, still moves it on those steps, leaves it off the map when cancelled and keeps its departures marked Cancelled. While the feed is fresh, every trip with an update is Estimated (`docs/data-states.md`, rule 3); the Metro, which SIRI-ET doesn't carry, and every trip without an update stay Timetable only, and nothing is Live or Ageing. The run's log line adds the feed's kind, last message and freshness, the unmatched journeys, and each mode's share of its trips on the map with an update, to two decimals.

### The listener

The listener is `src/listener/`, a second entry point in the one package and a second deployable beside the app (ADR 0012, as ADR 0019 amends it), run as `node src/listener/main.ts` on Node's own type stripping. `listener.Dockerfile` builds it into a container image with only the folders its lint fence lets it and the SIRI-ET reader import (`src/listener/`, `src/siri-et/`, `src/timetable/`, `src/domain/`) and the packages they need (Effect, rhea). CI builds the image on every change and starts it without its settings, and it must stop with an error naming them.

It runs on Fly.io as the app `mylder-listener` (`fly.toml`, #218): one shared-cpu-1x machine with 256 MB in Stockholm (`arn`), beside the app's functions, never stopped for want of requests and always restarted, with TLS at Fly's edge and a health check on `/health`. `.github/workflows/listener.yml` deploys it from `main` whenever its image or `fly.toml` changes, and by hand, with `FLY_API_TOKEN`, a repository secret holding a deploy token scoped to that one app; without the secret the job deploys nothing and says so. The maintainer runs `scripts/deploy/listener-setup.sh <env-file>` once, on their own machine with `fly`, `gh` and Node logged in: from the Dataudveksleren settings in the env file and a token it makes, it creates the app, stages the listener's settings below as Fly secrets, sets `FLY_API_TOKEN`, and sets Vercel's Production `MYLDER_UPDATES=siri`, `MYLDER_UPDATES_URL=https://mylder-listener.fly.dev/table` and the same `MYLDER_UPDATES_TOKEN`, printing none of them. The Fly account runs on prepaid credits with no card for automatic billing, so it is suspended, not billed, when they run out.

Its settings come only from the environment, which only the host's secrets set. A missing or malformed variable stops it at start with an error that names the variable, never its value:

| Variable                  | What it holds                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `DU_CLIENT_ID`            | The Dataudveksleren service account's Entra ID client id                                                            |
| `DU_TENANT_ID`            | Its tenant id                                                                                                       |
| `DU_SECRET`               | Its client secret                                                                                                   |
| `DU_ADDRESSES`            | One AMQP address a dataset, `amqps://<host>/t-distribution-<dataset>/<subscription>`, separated by spaces or commas |
| `MYLDER_UPDATES_TOKEN`    | The token the table route asks for, at least 32 characters, the same as the tracker's                               |
| `MYLDER_APP_URL`          | The app's public URL, whose timetable routes give the box's stops                                                   |
| `PORT`                    | The port it serves on, 8080 when unset                                                                              |
| `MYLDER_LISTENER_VERSION` | The commit the image was built from, which the table names; the image sets it from its build, `dev` when unset      |

- **AMQP.** For each dataset, the listener opens one connection over AMQP 1.0 with TLS on port 5671 (`src/listener/amqp.ts`, with `rhea`, as the probe did). It gets an Entra ID client-credentials token for `https://servicebus.azure.net/.default`, puts it on the connection with a CBS `put-token`, and opens a receiver on the dataset's subscription once the broker accepts it. Every 15 minutes it gets a new token and puts it on every open connection; a connection that can't take it is logged and opened again. It settles each message on receipt, at most once, and lets the broker send at most 20 ahead, so a backlog of Movia's 1 MB messages holds at most about 20 MB in flight. A gzipped body is unzipped, never past the reader's 4,000,000-character limit (`src/listener/body.ts`): a body over it, zipped or not, is skipped and counted as too large. A message's received time is when the broker queued it (`x-opt-enqueued-time`), or else when it arrived, so a backlog read after a restart keeps its own times. The shell holds no logic: each message goes to the reader.
- **Reconnecting.** A connection that closes or fails is opened again after 1 s, then 2, 4 and so on up to 2 minutes, and from 1 s again once it is listening. When no dataset has sent a message for 5 minutes between 05:00 and midnight, Copenhagen time, every connection is closed and opened again. At night a quiet feed is left alone.
- **The box's stops.** The listener asks the app for its timetable manifest, `GET <MYLDER_APP_URL>/api/timetable`, and, when the manifest names a version other than the one it has, for that version's stops, `GET /api/timetable/<version>/stops`. It keeps the ids of the stops and platforms inside the manifest's box, without leading zeros. It asks at start, every minute while it has no stops, and every 15 minutes after. When the app can't be reached, it keeps the set it has and logs why. Messages that arrive before it has a set are read and held, and listed once it has one.
- **Memory.** The reader's caps bound it: 20,000 journeys and 4,000,000 characters a message ("The reader", above). The table is built at most once a minute and one copy is held, gzipped.
- **Credentials.** No log line and no answer carries one. The listener cuts the service account's id, tenant and secret, each subscription id, `MYLDER_UPDATES_TOKEN` and the latest 8 Entra ID tokens it got, two hours' worth, past the hour or so each one lasts, out of every line it logs. An error from Entra ID names only its status and code. Its tests run the loop and the route with fake credentials and read every line and body.

#### The route

The listener has one inbound port. TLS ends at the platform's edge, and the port serves two routes:

- **`GET /table`** answers only a request whose `Authorization` header is `Bearer <MYLDER_UPDATES_TOKEN>`. Without it, the answer is 401, with no body. Until the listener has the box's stops and a first table, the answer is 503 with no body and `Retry-After: 60`. Otherwise it is 200 with the update table as JSON, gzipped (`Content-Encoding: gzip`), in the shape and version `src/siri-et/table.ts` gives it. The table is built at most once a minute and served from memory in between. It carries the listener's heartbeat: `writtenAt`, when it was built, and `lastMessageAt`, the last message's received time, beside the listener's version and start time.
- **`GET /health`** needs no token and says only `ok`, once there is a table to serve, or `starting`.

Anything else, another path, a query or another method, is 404 with no body.

#### Its minute line

Every minute the listener logs one JSON line whose `message` is `Listener minute`:

| Field                      | What it says                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `datasets`                 | Each dataset's messages and their bytes this minute, by its number. A dataset missing at a weekday noon has gone quiet.                                            |
| `held`, `journeys`         | Journeys the reader holds, in the box or not, and those the table lists.                                                                                           |
| `tableBytes`               | The table's size as served, gzipped: about 112 KB at 09:56 on the recorded Saturday.                                                                               |
| `builtAt`, `lastMessageAt` | When the table served was built, and the last message's received time.                                                                                             |
| `timetableVersion`         | The version whose stops the box uses. Missing until it has one.                                                                                                    |
| `parseFailures`            | Messages skipped as malformed or too large, and journeys skipped for want of an identifying part, since the line before, as counted by the table the line reports. |
| `requests`                 | Table requests this minute: `served`, `refused` for a missing or wrong token, and `unavailable` before the first table. The tracker makes one a run.               |

Beside it, the listener logs when a dataset starts listening, when a connection closes and how long until it opens again, when every connection is reopened for a quiet feed, a failed token renewal and a failed read of the app's timetable.

#### How the tracker reads it

With `MYLDER_UPDATES=siri`, each tracker run fetches the table once (`readUpdateTable` in `src/tracker/updates.ts`). `MYLDER_UPDATES_URL` is the route's whole URL, `https://<listener>/table`, and `MYLDER_UPDATES_TOKEN` is the token. The fetch has a 3 s timeout, and the gzip is undone by the HTTP client. The table is checked against its schema. A timeout, a failed fetch, any answer but 200, a body that fails the schema (a table of another version among them) a table built over 10 minutes ago, or an answer over 8 MB unzipped (`TABLE_MAX_BYTES`, about nine times the recorded Saturday's 895 KB) is no table that run. Each is logged as a warning that names why, never with the token, and none fails the run: the trips stay or turn as rule 3 of [`data-states.md`](data-states.md) says. With `siri` and either variable missing, or a `MYLDER_UPDATES_URL` that isn't https (plain http only to this machine), each run logs an error and reads no table, so the token never travels in plain text.

## One call a minute

The tracker runs at most once a minute, whoever asked. It is one function on the app's host, and it may run on several instances at once, so the once-a-minute rule cannot live in memory. It lives in the store: the first instance to take the lease for the current minute slot does the work; any other instance that lands in the same slot serves the stored snapshot instead and never calls the API.

One tracker run does, in order:

1. Take the lease for this minute. If another run holds it, serve the stored snapshot and stop.
2. Check the budget level (below). If the month is spent, make no call: build the snapshot from the timetable and the stored history, so states age by the rules in [`data-states.md`](data-states.md), and stop.
3. Call `journeyPos` once for the city box, in the position mode decided on the first day. Count the call before sending it.
4. Every 15 minutes, also call `himsearch` once, for the disruption list. Count it.
5. Load the previous snapshot from the store, join the answer to the service day's timetable, and run the data-state rules for every trip the timetable says is on the map (underway, or waiting from 5 minutes before its first departure).
6. Store the new snapshot and the updated call counter. Return the snapshot.

A run that fails between steps 3 and 6 still counts its calls, and the stored snapshot stays the previous one, with its API health set to the failure.

### The call

- **Service**: `journeyPos`, JSON, the key as a query parameter, host `www.rejseplan.dk`.
- **Box**: Greater Copenhagen, lower-left 55.45 N, 12.05 E, upper-right 56.05 N, 12.70 E. This holds every line the prototype draws on the Danish side: the S-tog network to Køge, Frederikssund, Hillerød and Klampenborg, the Kystbanen to Helsingør, the regional trains to Roskilde, the Metro, the airport, and all the A-buses and S-buses. The east edge stops at the Øresund, so the Swedish side (out of scope) is never requested. The prototype's own routes span 55.46 to 56.03 N and 12.07 to 13.00 E, the last 0.3 degrees being the Øresundstog to Malmö.
- **How full it gets**: the timetable puts at most about 1,010 trips underway in this box at once on a June 2026 weekday (07:37), counting every trip with a call in the box, including those partly outside it; Saturday peaks at about 640 and Sunday at about 580 (measured on the Labs GTFS, `docs/timetable.md`). Fewer than that have a position inside the box at any moment, but the morning peak is close to the cap.
- **Products**: everything that runs on a line: 1 IC, 2 ICL, 4 regional, 8 other trains, 16 S-tog, 32 bus, 64 express bus, 128 night bus, 512 ferry (harbour buses count as Bus), 1024 Metro, 2048 light rail. Not 256 flexible (demand-responsive, no line to draw on) and not 4096 flight. As one bitmask that is 3839.
- **Lines and operators**: no filter. The box and products are enough, and a filter would hide a line the timetable knows.
- **Cap**: `maxJny` at its maximum, 1,000. The answer holds at most 1,000 trips and says nothing about the rest, so a full answer is a warning, not a result. If the first day's `CALC_REPORT` count at the morning peak reaches 950, split the box into a northern and a southern half and poll them on alternate minutes: each half refreshes every 2 minutes, and the Live limit follows the interval between a half's answers, as it does for alternating modes. That keeps the whole map at the same cost. Shrinking the box is rejected because trips would leave the live area while still on the map; two boxes a minute do not fit the budget.
- **Mode**: `positionMode` as decided on the first day (next section).
- **Timeout and retry**: 8 seconds, one retry on a network error or a 5xx, none on a 4xx. The retry counts as a call.

### Which position mode, decided on the first day

`journeyPos` carries no "reported" or "computed" flag and no time per position. The request's mode decides what comes back: `REPORT_ONLY` (only positions a vehicle reported), `CALC_REPORT` (a reported position where there is one, else one Rejseplanen computed) or `CALC`. One October 2026 test found `REPORT_ONLY` empty with a Labs key while `journeyDetail`'s last position worked. So the first morning with the key, at the peak (08:00 on a weekday), call both modes once and count trips per product class. With R the `REPORT_ONLY` count and C the `CALC_REPORT` count:

| Finding                      | Mode every minute                                              | What the map can say                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| R is at least 70 % of C      | `REPORT_ONLY`                                                  | Live and Ageing for reporting trips. A trip the timetable expects but the answer lacks is Timetable only, or Ageing then No signal if it reported earlier. Estimated only from Mylder's own timetable-plus-delay, where a delay is known from an opened stop or vehicle.                                                                                                             |
| R is between 20 % and 70 %   | Alternate: `REPORT_ONLY` on odd minutes, `CALC_REPORT` on even | The modes take turns by run, so each refreshes every two poll intervals: 2 minutes at 60 s polls. A trip in the `REPORT_ONLY` answer is Live, with the Live limit measured on the interval between report answers (two poll intervals), as [`data-states.md`](data-states.md) sets it. A trip only in the `CALC_REPORT` answer is Estimated. Both answers feed Ageing and No signal. |
| R is under 20 %, or zero     | `CALC_REPORT`                                                  | Every position is Estimated. The map has no Live or Ageing state at all, because a computed position never counts as Live. The page says so once, in the about text, not on every dot.                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Both modes empty or erroring | None for positions                                             | The city is Timetable only. A vehicle a person opens gets one `journeyDetail` call and its last position, shown as Estimated. Raise it with Labs the same day. This branch cannot carry the first public version.                                                                                                                                                                    |

The mode is one setting the server reads at start, and the snapshot names it, so the browser and the tests know which states can occur. A trip's state never depends on guessing the mode.

Per-trip delay in `REPORT_ONLY` and `CALC_REPORT` answers is a first-day fact: the answer may carry each trip's stops with expected times, or a path with progress. If it does, every trip in the answer gets a delay and its next calls from the answer itself; if not, only opened stops and vehicles do, and the rest run on the timetable.

## The snapshot

One answer, one cache key, the same bytes for every viewer and both languages. It carries codes and times, never translated text, so the browser localises everything. It names the timetable version it was built against, so the browser loads matching static pattern and stop files.

### Size

A 1,500-trip snapshot with these fields is about 450 KB as JSON and about 50 to 70 KB gzipped (measured on generated data with random positions; real data, with repeated stops and lines, should sit at the low end). A browser polling every minute pulls 3 to 4 MB an hour. The app host's free bandwidth, 100 GB a month, covers about 25,000 viewer-hours a month, more than the first public version will see. If the snapshot grows past 100 KB gzipped, the first saving is to send next calls as offsets from fetched and positions as progress only.

## The browser

### Polling

- The browser asks for the snapshot when the page opens, then just after each snapshot's next-due time: at next due plus a jitter of 1 to 4 seconds, drawn once per page load. So every browser polls at nearly the same moment, which is what a CDN wants: one miss, then hits.
- Only while the page is visible. When the tab is hidden, polling stops. When it returns, the browser polls at once if the snapshot it holds is past its next-due time, else at that time.
- On a failed poll (network error, timeout after 10 seconds, or a 5xx), the browser keeps the snapshot it has and keeps the clock running: dots keep moving on their timed segments and states age by the clock. It retries after 5, 10, 20 and then every 30 seconds. The Disconnected notice and its timing are in [`data-states.md`](data-states.md).
- On a successful poll that returns the snapshot it already holds (same fetched time, which happens when the tracker's run has not finished), it retries after 5 seconds, up to three times, then waits for the next due time.
- The poll sends no parameters. There is one snapshot for everyone.

### Opening mid-minute

A browser that opens at 10:42:37 gets the snapshot fetched at 10:42:03. It places every dot where its timed segments say it is now, 34 seconds in, not where it was at fetch time, and ages every state from the clock. It then polls at 10:43:03 plus jitter. So an opening page is never a minute behind. The browser takes the server's clock from the response's own date header, and keeps the difference to its clock, so a wrong device clock does not age every state at once. Until the first snapshot lands with that header, the page doesn't know the difference, so the stop panel waits for it: a stop's countdowns read "—", never "Now", and its source lines never say "may be out of date" (`useKnownClockOffset` in `src/map/live.ts`). The vehicle panel, list and city pulse still read the device's clock until then; an answer with no date header leaves the device's clock as the best there is.

### Moving between polls

The browser holds the pattern's path and the distance of each stop along it, from the static timetable files. For each trip it builds a timeline from the snapshot: progress at fetch time, then the progress of each next call at its expected time. Between two points the dot moves at constant speed along the path; before its first departure a Waiting trip stands still; after its last next call it continues at the last speed until the next snapshot arrives, and never beyond the pattern's end. The same rule serves every state: a Live dot and a Timetable only dot move the same way, and only their glyph differs.

When a new snapshot lands, each dot's target for the current moment comes from the new timeline. The dot eases from where it is drawn to that target over about one second, along the path when both points are on the same pattern and less than 500 metres apart, and with a short fade and a jump otherwise. A target that lies behind the drawn point by less than 150 metres is treated as jitter: the dot holds still until the new timeline catches up, so vehicles never visibly reverse at a bus stop.

The animation loop runs outside React and feeds the map layer directly, in the frames of the map's one frame driver (`src/map/frames.ts`), which moves the aircraft too (`docs/aircraft.md`); React renders the panels from the snapshot at the poll's pace.

### What ages where

The data-state rules are one pure module the server and the browser both run. The server runs it at fetch time to set each trip's state; the browser runs it every frame from the same fields (reported, seen, had realtime, source, API health and its start time, the poll interval) and the clock. So a Live dot becomes Ageing on the browser at the right second without a poll, and a whole city turns No signal 5 minutes into an API outage even on a browser that has stopped getting fresh snapshots.

## Caching

Three layers, each with one job.

| Layer       | What it caches                                                            | For how long                                                                                                                                               | Why                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The browser | Nothing. `max-age=0`.                                                     |                                                                                                                                                            | The snapshot's own next-due time drives the poll, not a browser cache.                                                                                |
| The CDN     | The snapshot, by the one cache key.                                       | Until the snapshot's next-due time, never less than 5 seconds. Stale for up to 30 seconds while a fresh one is fetched; up to 5 minutes if the app errors. | So a hundred browsers polling at the same second cost one request to the app, and the app's own hiccup serves the last snapshot rather than an error. |
| The store   | The latest snapshot, the previous one's history, the lease, the counters. | Snapshot until replaced; counters for the month plus a margin.                                                                                             | So every instance of the app, wherever the CDN missed, serves the same snapshot and only one of them calls Rejseplanen.                               |

The CDN's TTL is set per response, as the seconds left until the next-due time, using the host's own CDN header so the browser never sees it. The CDN caches per region and expires each region at the same wall-clock moment, so after each minute boundary the app may get one request per region; all but the lease holder answer from the store. The app's host runs its functions in one region (Stockholm is the closest choice), so there is one store and one lease to reason about.

The host's own data cache is not relied on for the once-a-minute rule. Its default is per instance and in memory; its shared form is regional and durable but documents no collapsing of concurrent misses. The lease in the store is what guarantees one call. The shared form may still wrap the snapshot read as a cheap first layer, as long as the lease remains the rule.

## The server store

### What must persist between polls

- **The previous snapshot's history**: per trip, its key, when it was last seen and last reported, its last reported position and progress, whether it has had realtime this service day, its last delay, and its journey reference, which an opened vehicle's `journeyDetail` asks with. A trip's history is kept until it has arrived at its last stop on its expected times, and its delay for 2 minutes more, while it stands there (`docs/screens.md`, "Stop panel"). Without it, no trip can be Ageing or No signal, and Timetable only cannot be told from a trip that dropped out.
- **The latest snapshot**, compressed, so an instance that did not build it can serve it.
- **The lease**: one key per run, named by the moment the run falls due, set only if absent, expiring after a minute.
- **The disruption list**: the last `himsearch` messages that belong to Copenhagen (`docs/screens.md`, "Disruptions"), with how many the answer held, when they were fetched and when the list was last checked, kept a month so a spent month still shows its last list.
- **The call counter**: one counter per calendar month, plus one per service (`journeyPos`, `himsearch`, `journeyDetail`, `multiDepartureBoard`, `multiArrivalBoard`) for the about page and the logs. Keyed by month, so there is no reset to get wrong; kept two months.
- **On-demand caches**: a stop's departure board, its arrival board and a vehicle's detail, for 60 seconds each, keyed by stop id or trip key.
- **The join's corrections**: by Rejseplanen's journey (`ZI@DA`), the trip an opened vehicle's `journeyDetail` showed it to be where the join's first guess was another. Each is its own entry, `<prefix>:corrections:<service day>:<n>`, numbered by that day's counter `<prefix>:corrections:<service day>`, so two corrections made at once both stay; a journey corrected again takes its latest entry. A correction the day already has is not added again, and a day keeps at most 100. Kept two days, and read by every run and every stop's board for the service days they join.

### What the store is

A free hosted Redis from the host's marketplace, reached through one small interface (get, set-if-absent with expiry, set with expiry, increment) with an in-memory implementation for tests and local runs. The free tier of the first candidate allows 500,000 commands and 10 GB of transfer a month and one database. One tracker run uses about seven commands and moves about 150 KB (read history, write snapshot and history, lease, counters), so a month watched around the clock is about 310,000 commands and 7 GB. An instance that is still warm skips the history read when its own last snapshot matches the stored one, which is most minutes. The second candidate's free tier, 30 MB and 30 connections, is too small for a 70 KB snapshot plus history with room to spare, so it is the fallback only.

### If the store is down

The tracker still runs, from memory:

- It uses its own last snapshot as history if it is warm, else none. With none, every trip in the answer gets its state from the answer alone and every trip not in it is Timetable only; the snapshot's store field says `unavailable`, and the page can say freshness history is missing.
- Without the lease, two instances could both call Rejseplanen in one minute. To bound that, the budget level drops to `stretched` (90-second polls) while the store is down, and each instance keeps a tally of the calls it made and adds them to the counter once the store is back. Each instance also refuses to make more than 1,800 calls in a calendar day on its own count, which is one day's share of the budget plus slack.
- On-demand calls stop, since their caches are in the store.

Store outages are expected to be rare and short; a long one is a reason to stretch the poll further by hand, not to redesign.

## The budget

The free key allows 50,000 calls a calendar month. Every request the app sends with the key counts as one, whatever the service and whatever came back: a timeout, an error, an empty list and a retry all count, and are counted before the request is sent. The counter in the store is the only truth; the host's logs are a cross-check.

### Calls per service

| Service               | When                                                                                                   | Month watched around the clock | Realistic month, 6 hours watched a day |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| `journeyPos`          | Once a minute while anyone watches, at the budget level's interval                                     | 44,640 (31 days at 60 s)       | 11,160                                 |
| `himsearch`           | Once every 15 minutes while anyone watches, inside a tracker run                                       | 2,976                          | 744                                    |

| `multiArrivalBoard`   | Only when a person opens a stop's Arrivals tab, one call for the stop (and its platforms), cached 60 s | Shares the slack above         | Shares the cushion above               |
| `journeyDetail`       | Only when a person opens a vehicle, cached 60 s per trip                                               | Shares the slack above         | Shares the cushion above               |
| Retries and failures  | Counted with the service that made them                                                                | Inside the figures above       | Inside the figures above               |
| **Total**             |                                                                                                        | **about 49,600**               | **about 22,000**                       |

A stop, its Arrivals or a vehicle that is opened when on-demand calls are stopped is served from the timetable plus the snapshot's delay for that trip, and its departures and arrivals carry their trips' data states. Metro departures and arrivals never spend a call: they show interval text from the planned times.

An opened stop asks for its board this way (`src/tracker/on-demand.ts`, `stop-board.ts`); its Arrivals tab asks for its arrival board the same way, and an opened vehicle's `journeyDetail` shares the same cache and limit:

1. A board kept in the store for the stop, under `<prefix>:on-demand:stop:<stop id>` (its arrival board under `<prefix>:on-demand:arrivals:<stop id>`, so either tab spends only its own call), answers while it is under a minute old, whatever the budget says now.
2. Otherwise a call is made only when the pacing allows on-demand calls (normal, with the cushion) and the minute's shared counter, `<prefix>:on-demand:minute:<minute>`, is at 10 or under once this call has added itself to it. The 11th in a minute answers from the timetable.
3. The call asks for every platform of the stop at once, for the next 30 minutes, and is counted with the month's calls before it is sent, as every call is. A stop whose departures are all Metro makes none.
4. Each departure is joined back to its trip by its journey reference (`join.ts`), else by its line and planned time, so the row keeps its trip's key and takes its data state from the snapshot. The expected time, the track and a cancellation are the board's; a row whose trip has no realtime on the map but has it on the board is Estimated. A departure that joins no trip, such as an Extra trip, is left out, as the map leaves it out.
5. The answer says where its times came from: `{ kind: 'rejseplanen', at }` with the call's moment, or `{ kind: 'timetable' }`. It is due again when the snapshot is, or when the board's minute runs out if sooner.
6. A failed call is logged, a quota error spends the month as a run's does, and nothing is kept, so the stop answers from the timetable and the next viewer may ask again.

Either way the answer carries the stop's messages: those on its board's departures, then the disruption list's that name the stop, each once.

A stop's arrivals, asked for only while its panel's Arrivals tab is open (`/api/stops/<id>/arrivals`), come from its `multiArrivalBoard` call by the same steps: the stop's platforms over the next 30 minutes, the stop's own cache entry for a minute beside its departure board's, the same minute's shared limit, and each arrival joined back to its trip by its journey reference, else by its line, where it comes from, its platform and its planned arrival. The expected time, the track and a cancellation are the board's, and a cancelled arrival stays, marked. A stop where only the Metro arrives makes no call, and opening Departures alone never spends an arrival call. When no call can be made, or it fails, the arrivals come from the timetable plus the snapshot's delays and say `{ kind: 'timetable' }`. Either way they carry the board's messages that concern the stop, then the disruption list's that name it.

An opened vehicle asks for its detail the same way (`trip-detail.ts`), under `<prefix>:on-demand:trip:<trip key>`, within the same minute's counter:

1. The call asks with the trip's journey reference, which the history keeps once a position answer has carried the trip. A trip no answer has carried, such as a Timetable only one or the Metro, makes none.
2. The detail's calls are matched stop by stop on planned times onto the trip the join guessed (`docs/timetable.md`, "The join"). When they fit another trip of the day instead, one of the same line first, the answer stays under the key asked for, the vehicle the viewer opened, with that trip's line, pattern and calls and its key as `planned`, and the correction is kept: the next run joins the journey to it and moves what earlier runs saw of the journey to it, and a stop's board joins it so. The open panel then moves to the trip the snapshot files the vehicle's journey under. Calls that fit no trip answer from the timetable.
3. The expected time per call, the cancelled calls (a call is cancelled when every side it has is: the departure at the first stop, the arrival at the last, both between), the trip's cancellation (its calls stay, unmarked, with no times expected), Rejseplanen's own delay at the next call (`nextCallDelay`, expected minus timetabled) and the trip's own messages, each with the first and last of the trip's calls it covers, are the detail's. The state and the snapshot's `delay` stay the snapshot's, for the vehicle the viewer opened.
4. The source, the due time and a failed call go as a stop's do.

### The pacing rule

Each tracker run computes the **line**: 50,000 times the share of the month's minutes that have passed. The **gap** is calls spent minus the line. Spending under the line is a cushion; over it, the month is running ahead.

| Level                | When                                                          | Poll interval | `himsearch` | On-demand calls | The page                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `normal`             | gap at or under −100                                          | 60 s          | 15 min      | Allowed         | Nothing extra.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `normal`, no cushion | gap between −100 and 0                                        | 60 s          | 15 min      | Stopped         | Stop and vehicle panels say "From the timetable" where a call would have been made.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `stretched`          | gap over 0                                                    | 90 s          | 15 min      | Stopped         | A line in the about panel and the City pulse header: "Updating every 90 s to stay within the data budget."                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `tight`              | gap over 1,500                                                | 120 s         | 30 min      | Stopped         | The same line with "2 min".                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `spent`              | spent at or over 49,500, or a 4xx that says the quota is gone | None          | None        | None            | The Paused notice on every screen: "Live data is paused until 1 November: this month's calls are used up." States age as in [`data-states.md`](data-states.md): trips with realtime turn No signal after 5 minutes, and from the next service day the city is Timetable only. The Disruptions screen keeps its last list with its fetch time. |

The numbers work out so that a month watched around the clock at 60 seconds stays `normal` (it spends 1.07 calls a minute against a line that grows 1.12 a minute), and the cushion it builds, about 70 calls a day, pays for a few opened stops and vehicles. A realistic month builds a cushion of hundreds a day, so on-demand calls are almost always allowed. The 500 calls held back before `spent` cover the counter being a few minutes behind a store outage.

On-demand calls are also rate-limited on their own: at most 10 a minute across all viewers, whatever the cushion, so one busy evening cannot drain a week.

The level only moves at a tracker run, and the snapshot carries it, so every browser shows the same level at the same time.

### What the page shows

The budget level and the API health are two different things on the page:

- The budget level is a page-wide note, worded as above, in Perron's notice style, never on a dot. Below `spent` it changes no data state.
- The API health reaches the data states through the rules: after 5 minutes of `error`, `empty` or `spent`, every trip that had realtime is No signal, since the time the health changed. Before those 5 minutes nothing changes, so a single failed minute is invisible. `error`, `empty` and `spent` are the page condition Paused, whose notice and timing are in [`data-states.md`](data-states.md).
- The Disconnected notice is the browser's own, for its own failed polls, and is separate from both.
